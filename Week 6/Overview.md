The notebook also says that "data don't tell a story, but they become something from which a story can be told." I think that connects to the question about the space between counting and the things being counted. A count of graves only includes what was found, recorded, and put into the dataset. People who were never given a headstone, or whose stones can't be read anymore, wouldn't show up at all.

I also read through Dr. Graham's chi-square walkthrough. The idea is that the "monstrous equation" is really just a series of small steps. One thing stuck with me: if you collect ten times more data, the chi-square number gets ten times bigger even if the pattern hasn't changed. A bigger number looks like a stronger result when it's really just more data. This is close to Hacıgüzeller's question of whether more is genuinely more, and it applies to my Spotify data too. Thousands of plays could make a pattern look important without telling me anything more about how I was listening.
