### Week 7 — Networks

Building a network from the family cemetery data showed me how much the result depends on decisions made before any calculation. Catherine appears twice in the data, once in the Hayes family and once in the Miller family, with the same dates. Whether she is counted as one person or two decides whether those two families are connected at all.

The uncertain headstones had a similar effect. With only the stated relationships, there are three separate groups. With the uncertain ones added, the Clark and Wilson families join together. Brughmans makes the point that archaeological data are already the product of selection, and that the type of network we assume has to be justified (2010). The cemetery exercise showed this on a small scale.

A network diagram can make uncertain information look certain, since a question mark in the original data becomes an ordinary line between two people. On the other hand, building the edgelist made me read the data more closely, and I only noticed the two Catherines because I had to decide what each connection was.

I have seen something similar in my Greek and Roman Studies classes with family trees of Roman emperors, which involve adoptions, remarriages, and people known by more than one name. Each line in those trees also depends on a decision about the sources.

This could apply to my Spotify project as well. If I treat my listening as a network of devices and music, any connection I guess from memory would look the same as one recorded in the data, so I would need to mark the difference.
