This week's material was about GIS and how maps are used in archaeology. One thing I found interesting was that maps can show patterns in evidence without necessarily explaining them.

In the Linlithgow exercise, I used Google Colab to produce a map of burials from a medieval cemetery. The different colours represented different dates. Although I could see how the burials were arranged, I wasn't sure what conclusions could be drawn from their locations alone.

This reminded me of Hacıgüzeller's reading, particularly her question about whether producing more maps necessarily means having more knowledge (2024, p. 81). A map might contain a lot of information while still leaving important questions unanswered.

I think there is a similar problem with my Spotify project. I have records of what I listened to and when, but these don't necessarily tell me much about the circumstances of listening, particularly which devices I was using. I still need to work out how much of this information I can recover from the data itself and how much will require other sources.
