The main theme this week was how maps are used in digital archaeology and how they influence our understanding of the past. Geographic Information Systems (GIS) allow archaeologists to organize and visualize spatial information, but the way this information is represented can also affect how it is interpreted.

In Archaeology, Crafting Maps and Political Change, Hacıgüzeller discusses how maps are not simply neutral representations of archaeological evidence. The decisions involved in making them can influence what information is emphasized or left out.

I think this also relates to my personal dataset project. Spotify provides a record of my listening history, but the information it records does not necessarily represent the full experience. In both cases, digital representations depend on choices about what information to include.
